Execution Roadmap

Phase 1: Beachhead (Years 1-3)

Investment: £500m-1bn

  • Single import terminal (Teesside or Milford Haven)
  • 20-year supply contracts with Middle East producers
  • 3-5 major industrial anchor tenants in Northern England
  • Dedicated pipelines to first customers (10-20km each)
  • Target: British Steel, Tata Chemicals, INEOS, Essar

Phase 2: Scale Supply Chain (Years 3-5)

Investment: £2-3bn

  • Second terminal for geographical redundancy
  • 500,000 tonnes/year capacity
  • Pipeline network connecting industrial clusters
  • Salt cavern storage (30-day buffer)
  • International supplier diversification
  • Begin 10-20% hydrogen blending in gas grid

Phase 3: Own the Customer (Years 5-8)

Investment: £3-5bn

  • Launch consumer brand for residential market
  • Target new builds and pilot towns for full conversion
  • 50-100 HGV refueling stations on major routes
  • Fleet management services beyond fuel
  • Hydrogen-capable peaker plants for grid flexibility

Asset Sweating

Why Hydrogen Beats Electrons

Three Structural Advantages

Storage Economics

Seasonal energy storage is impossible with batteries. Hydrogen in salt caverns: £2-5/MWh storage cost vs Battery: £150-300/MWh. We dominate on 3+ month time horizons.

Energy Density

International energy arbitrage only works with hydrogen. Hydrogen ships carry 1,000x more energy than battery ships could.

Infrastructure Leverage

We repurpose the existing gas grid (£300bn of sunk infrastructure) rather than building new electrical distribution. Pure asset leverage play.

Why This Isn't Being Done Already

It should be obvious, right? But:

  • Incumbents can't pivot: Shell has $300bn in O&G assets. Shareholders revolt if they strand them.
  • Utilities are regulated: Can't take entrepreneurial risks. Everything goes through 18-month regulatory review cycles.
  • Infrastructure funds want proven models: They buy assets, not build new markets.
  • VCs don't understand energy: Too capital intensive, too long time horizon.
  • Industrials are customers: They want to buy hydrogen, not build supply chains.

The gap: No one has the combination of energy market understanding, infrastructure building capability, entrepreneurial speed, risk capital access, and willingness to build pre-demand.

Risks & Mitigations

Technology Risk

Hydrogen embrittlement, efficiency losses

Mitigation: Start with new pipelines to industrial customers. Test and iterate before residential rollout.

Price Risk

Hydrogen stays expensive

Mitigation: Long-term fixed-price supply contracts. Natural gas price spikes improve our relative economics.

Regulatory Risk

Government backs electrification instead

Mitigation: Diversify across industrial, transport, and residential. Can't lose all three bets.

Demand Risk

Customers don't switch

Mitigation: Heavy discounts to anchor tenants. Once they've converted, switching costs are prohibitive.

Supply Risk

International suppliers flake

Mitigation: 3+ suppliers across 3+ continents. Domestic production optionality (expensive but possible).

Stranded Asset Risk

Wrong technology wins

Mitigation: Stage investments. Only commit next £1bn after previous phase proves out. Option value approach.
